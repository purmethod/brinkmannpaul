# Feel That You Are Mortal

> Status: **Buchtext steht.** Pauls These: „Memento mori“ (erinnere dich) wird ersetzt durch „feel that you are mortal“ (fühle es).

## Kernaussagen (Paul)

- Nicht: Erinnere dich, dass du sterben wirst. Sondern: Fühle, dass du sterblich bist. Im Körper, nicht nur im Kopf. Wenn du das tust, verändert sich alles.
- Der Weg dorthin geht nur über den Körper: Kälte, Atem, Stille, Belastung, Fasten.
- Das heißt nicht, den Körper zu zerstören, um es zu beweisen. Du pflegst ein Ding, von dem du weißt, dass du es nicht ersetzen kannst.

## Buchtext (EN)

The ancients had an answer for the man on the second floor. *Memento mori.* Remember that you must die. Whispered to Roman generals during their triumphs, to keep them human at the peak.

It does not work.

Because **remember** is a word about thinking. And thinking cannot touch this. A man can think about his mortality for thirty seconds, nod at how profound it is, feel a small pleasant shiver of depth, and go back to his day completely unchanged. He added a concept to a mind that already collects concepts. Nothing moved.

You cannot think your way out of feeling like a god. The intellect is the wrong tool, because the intellect is on that floor with him and it works for him.

So I am changing the instruction.

**Not: remember that you will die.**
**Feel that you are mortal.**

Feel it in the body, not the head. Feel that this thing carrying you around is temporary and finite and closer to its end than you find comfortable. Feel that it can stop. Feel that everything you built, the company, the influence, the hundred employees, the rooms that go quiet, continues without you approximately as long as it takes to find a replacement.

That is not a thought. That is a sensation, and it lands where no argument can reach.

### How You Actually Get There

Only through the body:

**Cold.** Get into ice. Not for the metrics, but for the moment your system understands, without your permission, that it is a fragile thing that can be shut down by water at a certain temperature.

**Breath.** Go far enough into breathwork to feel the thread. Everything you are is riding on a rhythm you did not start and will not choose to end.

**Stillness.** Sit long enough that the noise drops and what is left is a body, breathing, running out. Meditation is not relaxation here. It is the only place quiet enough to hear that.

**Load.** Train to the edge, where the body reports its limits honestly instead of through the story you tell about it.

**Food.** Fast long enough to feel your dependence. You are three weeks from nothing, always, and a full fridge hides it from you.

Each of these does the same thing: takes the question out of the mind, where it can be admired, and puts it in the tissue, where it cannot be avoided.

### What Surprises Men

You would expect this to make life smaller. Darker. It does the opposite, violently.

When you feel, actually feel, that you are mortal, the world comes back on. Colour returns. The light on a wall in the afternoon becomes almost unbearable. The body you have been using as a machine becomes a miracle you are renting. An ordinary morning with ordinary coffee becomes something you would not trade.

**Permanence is what makes things invisible.** A man who unconsciously believes he has forever cannot see anything: it is all just there, available, uninteresting. The moment the clock becomes real in his body, everything in front of him becomes vivid.

And you cannot look down at a man you know is mortal in exactly the way you are. Ground floor to penthouse, same schedule.

One thing: feeling this does not mean destroying the body to prove it. That is the same ego in gym clothes. It means treating the body as what it is: finite, borrowed, not yours forever. **You take care of a thing you know you cannot replace.**
